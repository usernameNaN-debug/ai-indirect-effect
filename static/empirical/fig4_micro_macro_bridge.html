If chatbot users had really stopped paying preparers, the IRS would have noticed

Survey answers about “doing less” can be scaled up. Start from all filers, keep those who use chatbots, then those who use them for money and taxes, then those who say they hire a professional less. If each of those answers were a full switch away from paid preparation, the number of professionally prepared returns would have fallen sharply in 2026. It rose slightly.

Read literally, the survey implies a drop of roughly 9% in professionally prepared returns. The IRS counted 74.7 million such returns by May 1, 2026, up 0.3% on the year before. In the three previous seasons the same comparison ran between −0.1% and +2.1%. Other shocks moved the 2026 season too: refunds jumped 18% after tax-law changes and IRS Direct File was shut down. Still, a drop four times larger than any recent season's swing would be hard to hide.

The reconciliation sits in the last slider. If fewer than about one in eight “hire a professional less” answers were full exits, the implied change falls inside the noise of a single season. Most answers more likely describe a smaller purchase, one question not asked, or a check done with a chatbot instead of a call. Those are intensive-margin changes, which the survey cannot size and the IRS counts cannot see.
