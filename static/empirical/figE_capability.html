Frontier models now compute most test returns exactly right, but only with search, and still not all of them

TaxCalcBench gives a model every fact needed to complete a federal return and checks whether it produces the correct tax. A return counts as correct only if every evaluated line matches. This is a harder standard than answering a tax question, and closer to what a filer relies on when a chatbot fills in a form.

Share of test returns computed exactly right

Test set

Source: Column Tax, TaxCalcBench leaderboard, retrieved October 2026; original benchmark in arXiv:2507.16126 (July 2025).

When the benchmark launched in July 2025, the best model computed 32% of the 2024 test returns exactly right. Models released since then reach 63% on the same set. On the harder 2025 set, which uses realistic document inputs and state returns, the best models reach 70% to 78% when allowed to search the web, and roughly half that without it.

For the top models with search, 86% to 94% of individual lines are right, so the errors are concentrated: most of a return is right, and one or two lines are wrong. That is the pattern a filer is least likely to catch on their own.
